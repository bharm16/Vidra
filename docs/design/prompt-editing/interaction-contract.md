The October 14 approved removal supersedes earlier presentation requirements for the 39 retired screens. Do not rebuild draft comparison/management/discard screens, context or coupled-setting approval gates, technical attachment/storage/renderer repair screens, billing/checkout/credit receipts, extra retry confirmation, or the retired result notices. Unfinished work is reached through conversation history. Retry is a direct deliberate action with a new execution identity; uncertain acceptance still reconciles the original identity. Data retention, conflict detection, media ownership, idempotency and internal recovery remain required. The retirement record, not older screenshots or state-model labels, determines active UI scope. ## Independent state

Keep separate identities for the active draft/revision, viewed media version, selected phrase and its baseline, accepted request/slots, video-edit revision/selection, and timeline visibility. A change to one is not a transition of all the others.

Normal ongoing creation always retains the sidebar and a viewed asset where one is available. Only a genuinely empty workspace, unavailable/denied media, or an intentionally different narrow-screen view may omit it. An empty new draft does not clear a previously viewed asset.

Video creation submits through one primary button labeled **Make video** (image creation: **Make image**). Its chevron control changes the action; compact model, duration, shape and resolution chips open their settings. There is no additional **Make video** submit button, manual text resize grip, or visible undo/redo arrow pair in the composer. A genuinely empty conversation puts the composer in the main workspace instead of showing an empty history pane. User message bubbles and their text align to the right; assistant messages and media results remain left-aligned. Sidebar navigation, conversation-list items, Help and the account name are all left-aligned, not centered. The conversation header retains only **+**; unfinished requests remain accessible through past conversations and their drafts menu.

Empty first-use and new-request states omit the project name/selector and the “What are you making?” heading. An empty follow-up draft with retained history or viewed media is still ongoing work. Viewer, selected-asset and media-sheet headers omit routine asset-name/version labels; input selectors, media-library choices and timeline clips retain the identity needed to choose the correct media. Never render the “Saved; add to conversation” notice, attachment-repair cards, or instructions to repair an original destination on any screen. Track delivery and attachment facts internally; user-facing views show the media actually available.

All composer occurrences on Pages 24–28 use the approved two-tier design: attached media actions above the writing surface, source-style icon pills along its lower edge, and the existing submit action. The compact style comes from `419:445`, with chip styling from `419:415`; the wide source is `390:1287`. Preserve qualified controls, availability and actions rather than copying unsupported controls from the reference. The default desktop compact composer is **336 × 316px**, matching the `1071:90476` reference. The touch layout uses a **336 × 352px** base with 44px action targets. Additional explicit-input or settings rows may increase height; do not clip controls to force every state into 316px. Compact settings wrap above the full-width submit button in model, shape, duration, resolution, sound and action order, with model-specific advanced controls retained. Reference glyph geometry is scaled to its icon box and centered in the tile. Dropdowns anchor beneath their actual triggers, and history viewports reserve space above the fixed-size composer.

Use the canonical composer `1175:109219` at compact or wide dimensions, with its Media, Prompt and Controls slots. The shared controls family is `1236:91199` (stacked or inline). Controls must remain fully visible: their rows hug content, their ancestors do not clip them, and the body retains 20px of bottom padding. The prompt fills the remaining space and may scroll. Conversation headers use `1297:91713`. Every viewer/editor toolbar on a screen must be a linked instance of the full toolbar `1379:84830`. Its nested transport is `1347:84311`; editing actions use `1297:91738` or compact `1381:84770`, and seek positions use `1367:84664`. These nested components are not separate visible toolbar rows. The old playback family `887:84918` is retired from current screens. Do not rebuild detached per-screen copies of these layouts.

The full toolbar uses the shared Vidra appearance in one horizontal row, 40px high on desktop and 52px for touch. Primary controls use the white action token with dark foregrounds. The shared masters own intrinsic auto-layout sizing, spacing and styling; do not recreate controls or assign per-screen fixed toolbar widths. Playback, **Use**, Download, and applicable Export/timeline actions belong inside this component. Do not render a separate Export edit action, export/timeline strip, or two-control Use/Download mini-toolbar. Still images omit Play, Time, Seek and Volume; the full component shows Fullscreen, Use and Download when permitted. Stopped video shows zero elapsed time. Ended video shows its full duration, a completed seek position and Replay. Action availability must reflect the selected source and available outputs; common controls do not authorize unsupported actions. Collapsed editor previews omit Add and Undo, and canvas ratio belongs in output settings. Desktop preview media fits within 640 by 360 pixels while preserving aspect ratio; explicit full-screen views are exempt. Preserve playback state, full timestamp data, audio availability, and seek progress when changing presentation. Output canvases have no decorative outline. T15/T16 are square-format states of the same video editor with its timeline shown/hidden, not separate application destinations. The [full-toolbar standardization record](../full-toolbar-standardization-2026-10-13.json) supersedes the earlier rollout audit. The October 14 [critique remediation](../design-critique-remediation-2026-10-14.json) supersedes that earlier unfinished verification. Its geometry, component-link, contrast and render checks are design evidence, not application-code implementation.

After creation acceptance, D03/D11 open the specified new draft; the execution snapshot is immutable. W07 illustrates inspection of that immutable snapshot, not a requirement to lock the entire workspace while work runs. Video follow-ups start without an implicit input or edit operation. Image and multi-output follow-ups retain D04/D05's distinct rules; the deferred dedicated image editor does not rewrite those rules.

Current interface text uses Inter at 12px or larger. Phrase categories also have non-color cues: a single underline for subject, a wavy underline for motion, a dotted underline for lighting, and a heavier underline for environment. Keep the readable category label and an accessible category name; decoration never changes the words. Desktop navigation has an explicit active destination and hover/focus labels. Mobile screens use the canonical 64px navigation component, with controls and their hit areas at least 44 × 44px.

A second or later phrase edit uses the same state machine with the new draft revision and exact new range. It must preserve earlier edits outside that range. Duplicate words, Unicode/grapheme boundaries, punctuation, whitespace, long replacements and response-list changes are parameterized acceptance cases, not permission to use a fuzzy first match.

Enter on a focused option applies it once. Arrow keys navigate the option list with visible focus and keep the focused option in view. Escape closes without applying. Enter in the custom textarea inserts a line break; Suggest submits that text request. Tab reaches close, options, custom text and Suggest without a keyboard trap. Ordinary undo belongs to the focused text field; timeline undo applies only when the timeline editor owns that command. Opening or receiving a panel response does not move focus to an unrelated control.

## Timeline visibility

Visibility is a view preference, stored per user/browser/workspace/video-edit identity. It does not create an edit revision or dirty state. Collapse and expansion preserve exact media versions, track presence, timing, gains, captions, playhead, playing/paused state, selected elements, zoom and scroll. The preview fits the available area without changing fit/crop policy or output aspect ratio. Square/portrait fitting must use both width and height constraints.

The editor controls form one compact dock fixed to the viewport, independent of canvas panning and scrolling. Omit the edit title from workspace chrome. Also omit redundant conversation headings, standalone Drafts controls/menus and Original request controls/panels; unfinished work remains accessible through conversation history. Use and Download belong within the full shared toolbar, never an independent two-control strip. The dock stays reachable with the timeline shown or hidden and must not obscure editable tracks; Figma's fixed-child setting records the prototype behavior.

Center the intrinsic-width toolbar in a transparent horizontal auto-layout container aligned to its preview/editor pane. A change in visible controls must recenter the toolbar automatically; do not retain a manually positioned offset from an earlier toolbar width. Preserve the fixed/scrolling position when adding its centering container.

In the expanded sidebar, Create, Media and Video edits have no extra left padding inside their rows. Their icons share the conversation list's 20px left inset; all three active-state variants use the same alignment.

The toggle stays reachable when collapsed. Keyboard focus remains on the toggle; hidden controls are not focusable. A modal retains command ownership, and an unfinished drag must complete/cancel before collapse is applied. Explicit operations that require the timeline reveal it before focusing the relevant control. Inspecting another asset retains the edit and chosen visibility and provides Back to edit. Playback ownership transfers deliberately between the inspected source and edit preview; closing the timeline alone does not pause it.

No editor is implicitly created just because a video was generated. A silent file has no source-audio lane. Music/captions appear only when added, and waveform loading does not imply missing audio. The rich mobile example is a separate twelve-second edit fixture with deliberately added tracks; the desktop fixtures include silent 5.1-second, sound-bearing 8-second and mixed 13.1-second edits.
